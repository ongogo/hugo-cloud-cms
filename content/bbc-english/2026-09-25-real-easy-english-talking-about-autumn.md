| **autumnal** | related to autumn | 秋天的；秋季的 | 形容秋天的颜色、氛围 |

## 课文重点解析

### 1. 秋天的自然变化

> **Neil**: *"The leaves fall. The leaves fall off the trees and they are **crunchy** on the ground. You can make big piles of leaves and jump into them."*
> 
> **Neil**: *"树叶落下。树叶从树上掉下来，地上**咔嚓作响**。你可以堆大堆树叶跳进去。"*

> **Becca**: *"Before the leaves fall off the trees, they turn into this beautiful orange-reddish colour. And I love all of these **autumnal** colours."*
> 
> **Becca**: *"树叶落下前，会变成美丽的橙红色。我爱这些**秋天的**颜色。"*

**关键点**：
- ✅ **crunchy** = 干脆的、咔嚓响的（形容干叶子、苹果）
- ✅ **autumnal** = 秋天的、秋季的（形容词）
- ✅ 英式英语用 **autumn**，美式英语用 **fall**

---

### 2. 温暖舒适的感觉

> **Neil**: *"**Cosy**. That's a fantastic word. Cosy is that feeling you have of being warm when things around you are probably a bit cold."*
> 
> **Neil**: *"**舒适/惬意**。这个词很棒。**Cosy** 是指周围环境有点冷时，你感到温暖的感觉。"*

> **Becca**: *"I'm very excited to start wearing jumpers and big boots. And... it's getting colder now so I want to be warm and **cosy**."*
> 
> **Becca**: *"我超期待穿毛衣和大靴子……天冷了，我想暖暖和和、舒舒服服的。"*

**关键点**：
- ✅ **cosy** = 温暖舒适、惬意（尤指外面冷、里面暖）
- ✅ **jumper** = 毛衣（英式），美式说 *sweater*
- ✅ 典型场景：寒冷天走进有壁炉的屋子

---

### 3. 英国秋天的节日：万圣节 & 篝火之夜

> **Becca**: *"I am excited for Halloween... but on the 5th of November is when we have **Bonfire Night**."*
> 
> **Becca**: *"我期待万圣节……但 11 月 5 日是**篝火之夜**。"*

> **Neil**: *"**Bonfire Night** is when we make a big fire, an outside fire, to celebrate and we stand around the fire. And actually it's more about **fireworks** these days."*
> 
> **Neil**: *"**篝火之夜**我们生大火、户外火堆庆祝，围着火站着。现在更多是看**烟火**。"*

**关键文化点**：
| 节日 | 日期 | 活动 |
|------|------|------|
| **Halloween** (万圣节) | 10 月 31 日 | 打扮、不给糖就捣蛋、恐怖氛围 |
| **Bonfire Night** (篝火之夜) | 11 月 5 日 | 篝火、烟火、烤土豆 |

> **Neil**: *"When I was younger and lived more in the countryside, we would go to a Bonfire Night and we would stand around the bonfire and maybe even cook food in the bonfire... **Baked potatoes**."*
> 
> **Neil**: *"小时候住乡下，去篝火之夜围着火，**烤土豆**——用锡纸裹土豆扔进火里。"*

---

### 4. 季节更替的感受

> **Neil**: *"Usually I'm not a big fan. I don't really like autumn because it means that the summer is over... But this year it was so hot, I've had enough of summer, so I'm very happy that it's autumn."*
> 
> **Neil**: *"通常我不太喜欢秋天，意味着夏天结束了……但今年太热了，受够夏天了，所以很高兴秋天来了。"*

> **Becca**: *"I've always loved autumn, but I am a really big lover of summer. But yeah, it has been far too hot this summer, so I welcome the autumn this year."*
> 
> **Becca**: *"我一直爱秋天，但也超爱夏天。今年太热了，欢迎秋天。"*

## 批判性思考题

1. **Autumn vs Fall 的跨大西洋差异**：英式 *autumn* vs 美式 *fall*，词源分别为拉丁语 *autumnus* vs 古英语 *feallan*。还有哪些季节/天气词汇存在英美差异？(*autumn/fall, autumnal/fall colors, spring/vernal*)

2. **Cosy 的文化内核**：*cosy/hygge/cozy* 反映北欧/英语文化对 "冬季生存策略" 的心理建构。如何用英语表达 *"candlelight, wool blankets, hot drinks, slow living"*？

3. **篝火之夜的历史政治学**：*Guy Fawkes Night / Bonfire Night* 纪念 1605 年火药阴谋失败。从反天主教庆典 → 社区烟火大会，如何用英语叙述 *"secularization, community cohesion, public safety regulation"*？

4. **季节情感的气候变化视角**：*"I've had enough of summer"* 反映极端高温改变季节偏好。如何用英语讨论 *"shifting seasonal baselines, heat anxiety, phenological mismatch"*？

5. **感官词汇的拟声拟态性**：*crunchy, crackling, rustling, crunch* 等拟声词如何构建秋天的听觉景观？(*onomatopoeia, sound symbolism, sensory vocabulary*)

## 完整对话记录

### Transcript

**Becca**: Hi Neil. How are you?

**Neil**: Hi Becca. I'm very well, thank you very much. Have you noticed that it's not summer anymore? It's autumn. How do you feel about that? What do you notice?

**Becca**: Yes, it is autumn in the UK and the weather's getting a little bit cooler, and the leaves are starting to fall from the trees.

**Neil**: You like autumn, don't you?

**Becca**: I love autumn! I'm excited for this episode because we're talking all about autumn today.

**Neil**: We are. And people need to know that they can go to our website, bbclearningenglish.com, to get a transcript and a worksheet to help them improve their English.

**Becca**: So, right now it is autumn in the UK. You might hear it as fall in other English-speaking countries. What changes happen in the UK during autumn?

**Neil**: The leaves fall. The leaves fall off the trees and they are **crunchy** on the ground. You can make big piles of leaves and jump into them.

**Becca**: It's so much fun. And I love also the **crunching** sound when you walk over those autumn leaves. Before the leaves fall off the trees, they turn into this beautiful orange-reddish colour. And I love all of these **autumnal** colours – so, **autumnal** being the adjective to describe something that relates to autumn.

**Neil**: Yeah. You really like **autumnal** things, don't you?

**Becca**: I do.

**Neil**: You like to wear different clothes?

**Becca**: I do, yeah. I'm very excited to start wearing jumpers and big boots. And, as you mentioned, it's getting colder now so I want to be warm and **cosy**.

**Neil**: **Cosy**. That's a fantastic word. **Cosy** is that feeling you have of being warm when things around you are probably a bit cold. So, it feels **cosy** if you go into a house on a cold day and there's a big fire, for example.

**Becca**: Yeah. So, I obviously love autumn. How does the changing of the seasons feel to you?

**Neil**: Well, usually... usually I'm not a big fan. I don't really like autumn because it means that the summer is over and I love summer and the long, late evenings. But this year it was so hot, I've had enough of summer, so I'm very happy that it's autumn.

**Becca**: Yeah.

**Neil**: How about you?

**Becca**: I feel the same. I've always loved autumn, but I am a really big lover of summer. But yeah, it has been far too hot this summer, so I welcome the autumn this year.

**Neil**: OK Becca. So, what kind of things do we do in the UK in autumn?

**Becca**: Well, I am excited for Halloween. It's not as popular in the UK as it is in the USA, for example, but I do love Halloween because I love things that are a little bit scary, and that's at the end of October. But on the 5th of November is when we have **Bonfire Night**.

**Neil**: Yes, **Bonfire Night** is when we make a big fire, an outside fire, to celebrate and we stand around the fire. And actually it's more about **fireworks** these days. So, **fireworks** are those very pretty explosions in the sky that we look at on special occasions like New Year and that kind of thing.

**Becca**: Yeah. So, it's usually quite noisy on the 5th of November, but the sky is lit up with beautiful **fireworks**. I can't remember the last time I did a **bonfire**. Do you celebrate Bonfire Night every year?

**Neil**: Well, I don't have a **bonfire** because I live in a city and it's not very nice for my neighbours, but when I was younger and lived more in the countryside, we would go to a Bonfire Night and we would stand around the **bonfire** and maybe even cook food in the **bonfire**.

**Becca**: Oh, OK. So, like toasting marshmallows or melting chocolate?

**Neil**: **Baked potatoes**.

**Becca**: **Baked potatoes**...?

**Neil**: Yeah.

**Becca**: I've never... I've never cooked a **baked potato** on a **bonfire** before.

**Neil**: Yeah, you wrap the potato in foil and then you put it in the fire, and then you wait. And then you open it and it's completely black.

**Becca**: That doesn't sound very tasty to me!

**Neil**: It's difficult to get it right.

**Becca**: Right... OK. That's a whole new thing. Alright, Neil, it's time to recap some of the vocabulary that we used during this conversation. And we'll start with one of my favourite adjectives, which is **cosy**. And that's the feeling of being comfortable and warm, especially when perhaps outside it's not so comfortable and warm.

**Neil**: Yeah. And we also heard **bonfire**. A **bonfire** might make you feel **cosy**. A **bonfire** is a large fire we build outside for festivals and celebrations.

**Becca**: Yes, I remember celebrating Bonfire Night as a child and we also saw **fireworks**. **Fireworks** are explosions in the sky, which make bright, colourful lights for everyone to watch.

**Neil**: We were also talking about the leaves that fall on the ground, and when you walk on those leaves when they're dry, it's **crunchy**. And **crunchy** describes the sound that those leaves make and also the sound when you bite into an apple.

**Becca**: And before the leaves fall, they are orange, and orange and red can be described as **autumnal** colours, **autumnal** being the adjective that describes things that are from autumn.

**Neil**: That's it for this episode of Real Easy English but join us again next week for another episode.

**Becca**: Yes, and you can find more episodes, plus this one and a free worksheet on our website, bbclearningenglish.com.

**Neil**: Bye for now.

**Becca**: Bye!
