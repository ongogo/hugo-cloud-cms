## 课文重点解析

### 1. 核心表达：How do you take your coffee?

> **"How do you take your coffee?" / "I take my coffee black/white."**
> 
> 你怎么喝咖啡？/ 我喝黑咖啡/白咖啡。

**关键点**：
- ✅ **take your coffee** = 喝咖啡的方式、偏好
- ✅ **black** = 不加奶（美式常说 *black coffee*）
- ✅ **white** = 加奶/加牛奶（英式常说 *white coffee*）
- ✅ 可扩展：*take your coffee with sugar, with honey, strong, weak*

---

### 2. 咖啡因相关词汇

| 词汇 | 含义 | 例句 |
|------|------|------|
| **caffeine** | 咖啡因 | *Too much caffeine makes it difficult to sleep.* |
| **decaffeinated** | 去咖啡因的（完整形式） | *I drank decaffeinated coffee for a while.* |
| **decaf** | 去咖啡因的（口语缩写） | *I'll have a decaf, please.* |

**对话片段**：
> **Neil**: *I've tried to cut down on caffeine. So, I... for a while, I only drank decaffeinated coffee, or **decaf**, as we call it.*
> 
> 尼尔：我试着减少咖啡因摄入。有一阵子我只喝**去咖啡因咖啡**，我们简称为 **decaf**。

---

### 3. 短语动词：cut down on / cut back on

> **"Have you ever tried to cut down on coffee?"**
> 
> 你试过减少喝咖啡吗？

> **"Cut down on... You mean have less?"**
> 
> 减少……意思是少喝点？

**关键点**：
- ✅ **cut down on sth** = 减少某物摄入/数量
- ✅ **cut back on sth** = 同义，略显正式
- ✅ 常用语境：*cut down on sugar, cut back on spending, cut down on smoking*

---

### 4. 咖啡偏好对话要点

| 话题 | Georgie | Neil |
|------|---------|------|
| **每日杯数** | 两杯（早/晚） | 两杯（早/晚） |
| **加奶偏好** | 白咖啡 | 黑咖啡 |
| **加糖** | 以前加糖，现在不加；偶尔加蜂蜜 | 不加 |
| **咖啡因敏感度** | 不敏感，晚餐后喝浓缩也能睡 | 敏感，喝多睡不着 |
| **喝咖啡时间** | 早上随时 | 早上 9 点左右（工作时间） |
| **尝试减量** | 未提及 | 试过喝 decaf 一段时间 |

## 批判性思考题

1. **咖啡文化对比**：中式"美式/拿铁/卡布奇诺" vs 英式"black/white"。如何用英语表达"我要一杯燕麦奶拿铁，少糖，去冰"？

2. **咖啡因敏感度个体差异**：Georgie *caffeine doesn't have a big effect on me* vs Neil *too much caffeine → can't sleep*。科学上 CYP1A2 基因型决定代谢快慢。如何用英语表达"我对咖啡因不敏感/很敏感"？

3. **decaf 的心理安慰剂效应**：Neil *I didn't miss caffeine at the time... but now I drink caffeine. I don't care.* 这种"尝试戒断→复饮"模式在习惯养成心理学中如何解释？(*habit loop, withdrawal, relapse*)

4. **蜂蜜咖啡争议**：Neil *Eurgh! Honey in coffee?* 体现饮食偏好的主观性。不同文化咖啡添加物有哪别？(*越南炼乳咖啡、土耳其咖啡加豆蔻、北欧加蛋清、印尼加黄油*)

5. **社交饮茶/咖啡仪式**：*We make each other coffee in the office* 体现职场社交粘合剂功能。远程办公时代，虚拟咖啡时间如何用英语表达？(*virtual coffee break, coffee chat, fika*)

## 完整对话记录

### Transcript

**Georgie**: Hi Neil. How are you?

**Neil**: I'm very well thank you, Georgie. Are you enjoying your coffee?

**Georgie**: I am, but it's got a bit cool now. I prefer my coffee hot, but... oh well! Are you enjoying yours?

**Neil**: I've finished mine.

**Georgie**: Oh, that's a shame.

**Neil**: It's a sad moment when I've finished my second coffee of the day, because that's it. I don't have more than two.

**Georgie**: So, we're talking about coffee in this episode, aren't we?

**Neil**: We are. But before we start, of course, everyone needs to know that there is a free worksheet on our website. That's at bbclearningenglish.com.

**Georgie**: Yes, they can also find a free transcript, can't they?

**Neil**: They can, yes, at the same address. So, Georgie, we're talking about coffee. How much coffee, or how many coffees if we're talking about cups of coffee – how many do you have a day?

**Georgie**: I usually have two, and I know that you do as well, because we drink them both in the office, don't we? We have one cup of coffee in the morning and one usually in the afternoon, when we need to regain some energy and alertness.

**Neil**: Yeah. And so, we all know because we make each other... At BBC Learning English, we make each other coffee, so we know how we take our coffee.

**Georgie**: Yes. How we take our coffee is how we like it – what we like to put in it. For example, sugar or milk. I know that you don't like milk in your coffee. You take your coffee black.

**Neil**: I do. I like black coffee and you take your coffee white and you used to have sugar, but you don't anymore.

**Georgie**: That's right. Occasionally when I'm at home I put some honey in my coffee to make it a bit sweeter.

**Neil**: Eurgh! Honey in coffee?

**Georgie**: Yes. Is that surprising?

**Neil**: I've never heard of that.

**Georgie**: Really?

**Neil**: Sugar, but not honey.

**Georgie**: But why would that be so bad?

**Neil**: Well, I don't know. It's just I've never heard of it.

**Georgie**: It's really good. I would recommend.

**Neil**: OK. So, Georgie, what happens if you don't have your coffee?

**Georgie**: Well, in the morning if I don't have coffee, I feel very grumpy and very tired. In the afternoon if I don't have a coffee, it's not terrible, but I would prefer to have my afternoon coffee. How about you?

**Neil**: Yeah, I need my coffee, especially in the morning, but I don't have my coffee first thing in the morning.

**Georgie**: Oh, interesting.

**Neil**: I have it, kind of, I don't know... work time – nine o'clock.

**Georgie**: Why is that?

**Neil**: I think it's too strong, too powerful. Or maybe just the way I drink it.

**Georgie**: What happens if you drink too much coffee?

**Neil**: Ooh, then it's difficult to sleep because of the caffeine. **Caffeine** is that chemical in coffee and tea that makes you feel awake and alert. But too much means you're too alert.

**Georgie**: I don't think that caffeine has a big effect on me. I don't think I have trouble sleeping if I drink too much coffee. It's strange. Yeah, because sometimes I'll have an espresso after dinner.

**Neil**: Really?

**Georgie**: Yeah. And I can still sleep.

**Neil**: That's crazy.

**Georgie**: Yeah. Lucky me.

**Neil**: Lucky you!

**Georgie**: Neil, have you ever tried to **cut down on** coffee?

**Neil**: **Cut down on**... You mean have less? No, I haven't tried to **cut down on** coffee, but I've tried to **cut down on** caffeine.

**Georgie**: OK.

**Neil**: So, I... for a while, I only drank **decaffeinated** coffee, or **decaf**, as we call it.

**Georgie**: OK. And how was that?

**Neil**: Well, I didn't miss caffeine at the time, I don't think. It lasted for a long time, but now I drink caffeine. I don't care.

**Georgie**: OK. Neil, it's time to recap the vocabulary we used in the conversation. We used the verb **take**. So, Neil, how do you **take** your coffee?

**Neil**: I **take** my coffee **black**, and I know you **take** your coffee **white**.

**Georgie**: Yes. We also heard **caffeine**, which is the chemical that is in coffee, which makes you feel more awake.

**Neil**: Yes, but you can also get **decaffeinated** coffee or tea – it means there's no caffeine. And we usually shorten that just to **decaf** – decaf coffee.

**Georgie**: Yes. We also heard the phrasal verb **cut down on**, which means you have less of something.

**Neil**: Yep. And you can also say **cut back on**. It means the same thing.

**Georgie**: OK. Well, that is the end of this conversation about coffee in Real Easy English.

**Neil**: Yes, and if people want to watch more episodes of Real Easy English, what should they do?

**Georgie**: They should go to our website, bbclearningenglish.com, where they can find a free worksheet to test what they've learned. Goodbye for now.

**Neil**: Goodbye!
